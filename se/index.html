What this means for IT service providers

The law reaches you through your customers' contracts

Whether or not your own company is covered, the customers of yours that are covered now have to look at their suppliers. This is what the texts say, and what it tends to produce on your side: questionnaires, contract amendments and requests for documents.

Covered customers must assess you.

Before buying a system or outsourcing information processing, a covered entity has to assess the risks and evaluate whether the intended supplier can meet the cybersecurity requirements set, for the whole term of the contract (MCFFS 2026:11, 4 kap. 1 §).

Existing contracts are being completed.

Agreements concluded before 1 October 2026 are to be supplemented with cybersecurity requirements where possible. The recommended contract content includes information about incidents, a right to terminate and the return of data at the end of the contract (MCFFS 2026:11, 4 kap. 1 and 4 §§).

Check your own status.

Managed services and managed security services are a covered sector, supervised by Post- och telestyrelsen (PTS), but the law applies subject to a size condition. We do not tell you whether your company is covered: read chapter 1 of the act or ask your legal adviser.

Sources: MCFFS 2026:11 (PDF) · Cybersäkerhetslag (2025:1506) · Cybersäkerhetsförordning (2025:1507), 7 §. This page is a summary for information, not legal advice.

Swedish legal framework

The texts, what they require, and where to read them

Status as read on 17 November 2026. Swedish terms and references are quoted as published; every card links to the official source, in Swedish. This is a summary for information, not legal advice, and it does not say whether your organisation is covered.

The act and the ordinance

Cybersäkerhetslag (2025:1506) and cybersäkerhetsförordning (2025:1507) have been in force since 15 January 2026. They implement the NIS2 directive in Sweden and replace the earlier act, lagen (2018:1174).

Sources: Cybersäkerhetslag (2025:1506) · Cybersäkerhetsförordning (2025:1507)

Who is covered

  • Entities in the sectors of annexes 1 and 2 of the NIS2 directive that are established in Sweden and are medium-sized or larger.
  • Municipalities, regions and certain public authorities.
  • A few categories regardless of size.

Source: Cybersäkerhetslag (2025:1506), 1 kap.

What covered entities must do

  • Register with the authority.
  • Take risk-management measures in ten areas: risk analysis, incident handling, continuity, supply chain security, acquisition and development, assessment of effectiveness, cyber hygiene and training, cryptography, personnel security with access control and asset management, and authentication.
  • Have the members of management trained, and inform management at least once a year (MCFFS 2026:11, 3 kap. 4 §).

Sources: Cybersäkerhetslag (2025:1506), 2 kap. · MCFFS 2026:11

Incident reporting deadlines

  • Early warning within 24 hours (2 kap. 5 §).
  • Incident report within 72 hours (2 kap. 6 §).
  • Final report within one month (2 kap. 8 §).

The detailed rules on reporting are in MCFFS 2026:8, in force since 1 July 2026.

Sources: Cybersäkerhetslag (2025:1506), 2 kap. · NCSC: list of regulations

Documentation under MCFFS 2026:11

  • An action plan (åtgärdsplan) recording the chosen measures and the risks they address (3 kap. 13 §).
  • Internal rules, and the information needed for supervision kept on record (3 kap. 3 §).
  • Information classification (3 kap. 11 §) and risk criteria (3 kap. 12 §).
  • Up-to-date architecture documentation, inventory and operations documentation (4 kap. 8–10 §§).
  • Recommended, not mandatory, standards: SS-ISO/IEC 27001:2022 and SS-EN ISO/IEC 27002:2022 (3 kap. 2 §).

Source: MCFFS 2026:11 (PDF) — decided on 15 June 2026, in force since 1 October 2026

Supply chain

  • Before a purchase or an outsourcing, the entity assesses the risks and makes sure the supplier can meet the requirements during the whole contract term.
  • Contracts concluded before 1 October 2026 are to be completed where possible.
  • Recommended contract content: information about incidents, right to terminate, return of data.

Source: MCFFS 2026:11, 4 kap. 1–4 §§ (PDF)

Managed service providers

Managed services and managed security services are a covered sector, supervised by Post- och telestyrelsen (PTS). For these providers the measures are set at EU level by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690. The size condition applies to them as well, so each provider has to check its own status.

Sources: Cybersäkerhetsförordning (2025:1507), 7 § · Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690

Sanctions

  • Administrative fine of at least SEK 5,000.
  • Essential entities: up to the higher of 2 % of total worldwide turnover and EUR 10 million.
  • Important entities: up to the higher of 1.4 % and EUR 7 million.
  • Public sector: up to SEK 10 million.

Source: Cybersäkerhetslag (2025:1506), 4 kap. 10 §

Regulations, authorities and guidance

  • MCFFS 2026:1 — registration, in force since 2 February 2026.
  • MCFFS 2026:8 — incident reporting, in force since 1 July 2026.
  • MCFFS 2026:11 — security measures and management training, in force since 1 October 2026.
  • MCFFS 2026:12 — security audits and scans, in force since 1 October 2026.

Since 1 July 2026 the cybersecurity activity of Myndigheten för civilt försvar (MCF) has moved to the national cybersecurity centre NCSC within Försvarets radioanstalt (FRA), which is the single point of contact and the CSIRT. The official guidance on applying MCFFS 2026:11 is announced for March 2027.

Sources: NCSC: Så ser NIS2-regleringen ut · Cybersäkerhetsförordning (2025:1507), 23 and 31 §§

Is this an audit or a certification?

No. We prepare and document. Certification, for example against ISO/IEC 27001, stays with an accredited body, and supervision stays with the Swedish authorities. We never promise "guaranteed compliance". Does the Swedish law apply to my company?

We do not answer that for you, and nothing on this page says that you are covered.
